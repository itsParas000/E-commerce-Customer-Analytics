# E-commerce Customer Analytics

## Project Overview

This project analyzes customer purchasing behavior for a UK-based online retailer using the **UCI Online Retail II** transaction dataset.

The goal is to understand customer value, purchasing behavior, repeat purchasing, returns, RFM segments, and customer inactivity so that businesses can better target retention and customer engagement activities.

## Business Problem

The company wants to:

- Identify its most valuable customers
- Understand repeat vs one-time customer behavior
- Identify customers who may be becoming inactive
- Segment customers based on Recency, Frequency, and Monetary value
- Understand customer return behavior
- Measure order frequency and customer value
- Translate customer-level analysis into actionable business insights

> **Important:** Customers with no purchase for more than 100 days are classified as **inactive** in this project. This is an observed inactivity rule, not confirmed customer churn, because the dataset ends on December 9, 2010.

## Data Cleaning

The following cleaning rules were applied:

1. Removed duplicate transaction rows.
2. Removed records with missing `Customer ID`.
3. Removed transactions with `Price <= 0`.
4. Kept negative quantities because they represent returns/cancellations and are relevant to customer analysis.
5. Kept missing product descriptions because customer-level analysis did not depend on the description field.
6. Created a `Revenue` column:

```python
Revenue = Quantity × Price
```

### Cleaned transaction data

- Rows after cleaning: **410,732**
- Customers with at least one positive purchase: **4,312**

## RFM Analysis

RFM analysis was used to evaluate customer behavior using:

### Recency

Number of days since the customer's most recent purchase.

**Lower Recency = better recent engagement.**

### Frequency

Number of unique orders placed by the customer.

**Higher Frequency = more repeat purchasing.**

### Monetary

Total historical revenue generated by the customer.

**Higher Monetary = higher historical customer value.**

Each metric was divided into five quintile-based scores.

- Recency scoring was reversed because lower recency is better.
- Frequency and Monetary scoring increased with higher values.

Customer segments were then created using RFM score combinations.

## Retention & Inactivity Analysis

Customer order dates were aggregated at the **customer + invoice level** before calculating the time between orders.

This avoided incorrectly treating multiple product lines from the same invoice as separate purchases.

### Order gap findings

- Median gap between orders: **20 days**
- Mean gap: **38.44 days**
- 90th percentile: **101 days**
- 95th percentile: **146 days**

Based on this distribution, customers with more than **100 days since their last purchase** were classified as inactive.

### Inactivity result

- Active customers: **2,994**
- Inactive customers: **1,318**
- Inactive rate: **30.57%**

Among the RFM segments, the inactive customers are concentrated in:

- **Hibernating:** 861
- **At Risk:** 457

## Key Business Findings

### 1. Repeat customers generate most customer revenue

- Repeat customers: **2,893 (67.09%)**
- One-time customers: **1,419 (32.91%)**
- Revenue from repeat customers: **£8.30M**
- Revenue from one-time customers: **£496K**

This indicates that repeat purchasing is strongly associated with customer revenue in this dataset.

### 2. A significant share of customers are inactive

- **1,318 customers (30.57%)** had more than 100 days since their last purchase.
- Hibernating customers account for **861** of these inactive customers.
- At Risk customers account for **457**.

### 3. Champions contribute the largest historical customer value

- Champions: **926 customers**
- Total historical customer value: **£5.71M**
- Average historical customer value: **£6,168.93**

### 4. One-time customers show lower recent engagement

Average days since last purchase:

| Customer Type | Average Days Since Last Purchase |
|---|---:|
| One-Time | 150.6 |
| Repeat | 60.5 |

Median days since last purchase:

| Customer Type | Median Days Since Last Purchase |
|---|---:|
| One-Time | 126 |
| Repeat | 34 |

This shows a clear difference in observed purchasing recency between one-time and repeat customers.

### 5. Returns affect a substantial share of customers

- Customers with at least one return: **1,729 (40.1%)**
- Customers with no return: **2,583 (59.9%)**
- Returned items as a share of purchased items: **3.82%**

The 40.1% figure is **customer incidence of returns**, while 3.82% is the **item-level return rate**. They measure different things.

### 6. Order frequency is strongly right-skewed

- Median orders per customer: **2**
- Mean orders per customer: **4.46**
- Maximum: **205**
- Skewness: **10.55**

Most customers placed relatively few orders, while a small number of customers placed very high numbers of orders.

## Business Recommendations

Based on the analysis:

1. **Focus retention efforts on inactive customers**, particularly Hibernating and At Risk customers.
2. **Encourage one-time customers to make a second purchase**, since repeat customers generate substantially more historical revenue.
3. **Protect high-value customers** through targeted retention and loyalty initiatives.
4. **Investigate return patterns** to understand whether specific products, orders, or customer groups contribute disproportionately to returns.
5. **Use customer segments for targeted marketing** instead of applying the same retention strategy to every customer.

---

## Important Analytical Limitations

- The dataset ends on **December 9, 2010**, so inactivity cannot be treated as confirmed long-term churn.
- Historical customer value is based on observed past revenue; this project does **not** claim to predict future Customer Lifetime Value.
- The 100-day inactivity threshold is a data-driven analytical rule based on observed order gaps, not a confirmed company policy.
- RFM segment definitions depend on the scoring rules used in this project.
- Revenue represents transaction value and should not be interpreted as profit.
